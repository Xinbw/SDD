\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

SDD makes semantic regions a controllable unit of client-private supervision.
The $KC$-coordinate report reuses one target across related examples, with
tight sensitivity $\sqrt{2K}$. A regional-label quality model connects geometry,
vote margins and privacy noise; paired decoding experiments identify the role
of class allocation. With strong frozen public features, 10k CIFAR-10 clients
and $\varepsilon=0.1$, taxonomy-level matching reaches $86.3\%$ without public
task labels and uses $200\times$ fewer coordinates than per-example transfer.
Natural clients and compact-head comparisons make the available supervision
resources explicit. Release granularity is a measurable design choice for
learning through protected aggregates.
