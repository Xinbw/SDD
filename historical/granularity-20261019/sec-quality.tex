\section{Release Resolution and Target Quality}
\label{sec:quality}

Choosing $K$ balances the task resolution supplied by public geometry against
the reliability of a noisy regional decision. We analyze the released target
itself, the common supervision subsequently combined with private examples.

\subsection{An exact geometry--decoding decomposition}

Fix a partition and a labelled diagnostic proxy. Let $w_k=|P_k|/|P|$,
$a_{kc}=\Pr_P(y=c\mid k)$, $a_k^*=\max_c a_{kc}$, and
$Q_{kc}=\Pr(\hat y_k=c\mid q)$ for clean mean regional votes $q$ and the
specified release/decoder. For any hard decoder, including coupled matching,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Acc}_{P}]=\sum_k w_k\sum_c a_{kc}Q_{kc}.
\label{eq:quality-exact}
\end{equation}
Writing $\operatorname{Pur}(K)=\sum_k w_ka_k^*$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
1-\mathbb E[\operatorname{Acc}_{P}]
=1-\operatorname{Pur}(K)+\sum_{k,c}w_k(a_k^*-a_{kc})Q_{kc}.
\label{eq:quality-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term is the impurity of constant regional labels; the second
measures the decoder's allocation among classes. It includes both clean
teacher disagreement with the regional majority and noise-induced changes.
Thus a flip probability alone is insufficient: an alternative class can
still be correct for part of a mixed region.

Purity bounds a constant hard-label classifier on this same proxy.
Final local models also receive private examples and make nonconstant
predictions, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:quality-exact} is a release-label model, with
local-model accuracy evaluated separately. Partitions fitted at different
$K$ need not be nested; both purity and teacher margins are measured for each
candidate rather than assumed monotone.

\subsection{Noise-supported regional margins}

For one release, the mean-noise coordinate is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
$v^2=nb^2/(s^2n^2)$, retaining exact integer calibration.
Let $c_k=\arg\max_c q_{kc}$ and $m_{kc}=q_{k,c_k}-q_{kc}$.

\begin{theorem}[Uncentered regional target bound]
\label{thm:quality}
For coordinate arg-max of the uncentered noisy vote matrix and $c\ne c_k$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(\hat y_k=c\mid q)\le\exp[-m_{kc}^2/(4v^2)].
\label{eq:margin-tail}
\end{equation}
Consequently the conditional expected proxy-label accuracy is at least
\begin{equation}
\sum_k w_k\left[a_{k,c_k}-\sum_{c\ne c_k}
(a_{k,c_k}-a_{kc})_+ e^{-m_{kc}^2/(4v^2)}\right]_+.
\label{eq:quality-lower}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Independent discrete-Gaussian coordinates have sub-Gaussian parameter $v$;
a competitor must overcome margin $m_{kc}$ with a difference of parameter
$\sqrt2v$. Chernoff's bound gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin-tail}; summing only
positive correctness losses gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:quality-lower}.
The theorem concerns unwrapped coordinate arg-max; simplex projection
preserves its winning coordinate. The modular wrap probability certified in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} supplies an additive error bound for ring arithmetic.
Centered decoding couples regions through the released prior;
matching couples them through assignment, so these decoders use the general
identity rather than the uncentered tail bound.

Without collusion, $v\approx\sqrt{TK/\rho_\varepsilon}/n$.
For two classes under continuous independent Gaussian noise, the clean winner
flips with probability $\Phi[-m_k/(\sqrt2v)]$.
For multiple classes, we simulate all competitors and the actual projection
and prior correction. This Gaussian approximation predicts $Q_{kc}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:quality-exact}; independent exact-noise trials check its accuracy.
When all relevant margins dominate $v$, decoding stabilizes. Further budget
then preserves regional targets; additional task resolution requires changing
the partition or target representation.

\subsection{Public-only resolution selection}

The deployment rule trains teachers on labelled public development examples,
forms their regional votes, and estimates $a_{kc}$ on public validation
examples assigned to the nearest public centroid. For each candidate $K$,
it simulates Gaussian noise at the actual deployment certificate's $v$,
applies centered decoding, and computes expected validation label quality
using validation region frequencies as weights.
It selects the largest expectation, breaking ties toward smaller $K$.
This directly evaluates the geometry--noise tradeoff without private-count
access. It is a public design surrogate, rather than an optimizer of final
local-model accuracy. The no-public-task-label condition instead fixes
$K=C$ and its decoder; benchmark labels enter offline diagnostics alone.
